## Syllabus  

### 1. Classical Mechanics (Weeks 1-4)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Kinematics and Dynamics of Motion  
  - Newton's Laws of Motion  
  - Work, Energy, and Power  
  - Momentum and Collisions  
  - Rotational Dynamics and Angular Momentum  
  - Oscillatory Motion  
- **Practice**: Solve problems related to motion, energy conservation, and rotational dynamics  
- **Applications**: Robotics, simulation of physical systems  

---

### 2. Thermodynamics and Statistical Physics (Weeks 5-6)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Laws of Thermodynamics  
  - Heat and Work  
  - Entropy and the Carnot Cycle  
  - Statistical Interpretation of Entropy  
  - Kinetic Theory of Gases  
- **Practice**: Analyze heat engines, solve problems involving entropy and statistical distributions  
- **Applications**: Energy systems, thermal management in electronics  

---

### 3. Computational Methods in Physics (Weeks 7-10)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Numerical Solutions to Differential Equations  
  - Monte Carlo Methods  
  - Simulation of Physical Systems (Mechanics, Electrodynamics)  
  - Data Analysis and Visualization  
  - Fourier Transform Applications in Physics  
  - Optimization Techniques in Physics Simulations  
- **Practice**:  
  - Write Python programs for simulations (mechanics, electromagnetism).  
  - Create visualizations using Matplotlib.  
  - Solve boundary conditions using numerical methods.  
- **Applications**:  
  - Scientific computing, control systems.  
  - Computational electromagnetics, ray-tracing.  
  - Data-driven analysis of physical models.  

---

### 4. Waves and Oscillations (Weeks 11-12)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Mechanical and Electromagnetic Waves  
  - Wave Equation and Speed of Waves  
  - Superposition and Interference  
  - Standing Waves and Resonance  
  - Fourier Analysis of Waves  
- **Practice**: Analyze wave phenomena in mechanical and electromagnetic systems  
- **Applications**: Signal processing, acoustics  

---

### 5. Electromagnetism (Weeks 13-17)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Electrostatics and Coulomb's Law  
  - Electric Potential and Capacitance  
  - Magnetic Fields and Ampère’s Law  
  - Electromagnetic Induction and Faraday's Law  
  - Maxwell's Equations  
  - Electromagnetic Waves  
- **Practice**: Solve problems using Maxwell's equations, analyze circuits and field interactions  
- **Applications**: Microprocessor functioning, electromagnetic shielding in devices, antenna design, and circuit optimization  

---

### 6. Optics (Weeks 18-19)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Reflection and Refraction  
  - Lenses and Mirrors  
  - Wave Optics: Interference, Diffraction, and Polarization  
  - Fiber Optics and Applications  
- **Practice**: Analyze optical systems using ray tracing and wave equations  
- **Applications**: Optical communication, imaging technologies  

---

### 7. Quantum Mechanics (Weeks 20-23)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Wave-Particle Duality  
  - Schrödinger Equation and Applications  
  - Quantum States and Operators  
  - Atomic and Molecular Structure  
  - Quantum Tunneling and Applications  
- **Practice**: Solve problems involving quantum behavior of particles  
- **Applications**: Quantum computing, semiconductor design  

---

### 8. Special Relativity (Weeks 24-25)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Postulates of Special Relativity  
  - Time Dilation and Length Contraction  
  - Relativistic Energy and Momentum  
  - Lorentz Transformations  
  - Applications in High-Energy Physics  
- **Practice**: Analyze relativistic effects in moving systems  
- **Applications**: Particle accelerators, GPS systems  

---

### 9. Solid-State Physics (Weeks 26-28)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Crystal Lattice and Unit Cells  
  - Band Theory of Solids  
  - Electrical and Thermal Properties of Solids  
  - Semiconductors, Transistors, and IC Design  
- **Practice**: Analyze semiconductor behavior and solid-state devices  
- **Applications**: Electronics, material science  

---

### Bonus: Physics of Computation (Optional Week 29)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Thermodynamics of Information  
  - Landauer's Principle  
  - Physical Limits of Computation  
  - Reversible Computing  
- **Applications**: Understanding computational limits in hardware and energy systems-e
