## Key Concepts and Laws  
- Physics is regarded as an experimental science
- Physicists observe natural phenomena and their effects and explain them using theories
- More well and widely used theories are called laws or principles
- A theory is based on accepted fundamental principles and observations, it does not imply it as unproven concept or a random thought
- Physical Theory undertakes the following steps:
	- Ask appropriate questions
	- Design experiments to answer those questions
	- Draw conclusions from the results of the experiment(s)
- No theory is regarded as the final or ultimate truth, you cannot "prove" that is always correct, you can only find situations where the said theory does not ideally hold and adjust it to fit those parameters or discard it entirely. The conditions for which the theory is said to be true is called the range of validity.
- ### Idealized Model
	- This is a simplified version of a subject which a theory is to be applied upon, like assuming a ball to be a point mass etc.
	- The goal of this is to give a general but relatively usable value from what we're solving, if we put in too much details, it soon looses it's everyday use.
	- We keep the important parts and assume the rest to be negligible
	- Validity of the predictions made is limited to the validity of the model
- ### Standards and Units
	- Physical quantities are numbers used to describe physical phenomena quantitatively
	- The explanation of a measure of a physical quantity is called an **operational definition**
	- Physical quantities are described by how we calculate from other fundamental quantities
	- Measuring a quantity references it to a given **standard** whose unary value is a **unit**. meter is the unit of distance, second is the unit of time
	- When using a number to represent a physical quantity or phenomena, always attach it's valid unit
	- Unit prefixes, kinda like size modifiers: $k, n, G, m$ etc
- ### Unit Consistency and Conversions
	- Equations must always be dimensionally consistent meaning units on one side must equal units on the other side. You can't add apples and oranges so adding a second to a kilogram wouldn't make sense, multiplication and division of units however may hold
	- Units should be treated just like algebraic symbols in equations in terms of operations and handling
	- Always include units in calculations
	- You can use **unit multipliers** for conversions (check textbook)
