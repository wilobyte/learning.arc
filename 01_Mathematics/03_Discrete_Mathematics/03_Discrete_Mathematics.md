## Syllabus  

### 1. Set Theory (Weeks 1-2)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Basic Set Operations (Union, Intersection, Complement)  
  - Venn Diagrams  
  - Set Cardinality  
  - Power Sets  
  - Cartesian Product  
- **Practice**: Solve problems involving set operations, Venn diagrams  
- **Applications**: Foundations of mathematics, database theory  

---

### 2. Logic and Proofs (Weeks 3-4)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Propositional Logic  
  - Logical Connectives and Truth Tables  
  - Logical Equivalences  
  - Quantifiers (Universal, Existential)  
  - Methods of Proof (Direct, Indirect, Contradiction, Induction)  
- **Practice**: Construct truth tables, write formal proofs  
- **Applications**: Computer science, reasoning in algorithms  

---

### 3. Relations and Functions (Weeks 5-6)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Properties of Relations (Reflexive, Symmetric, Transitive)  
  - Equivalence Relations  
  - Functions (Injective, Surjective, Bijective)  
  - Composition and Inverses of Functions  
- **Practice**: Solve problems involving relations and functions  
- **Applications**: Data structures, database management  

---

### 4. Algorithms and Complexity (Weeks 7-8)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Algorithm Design and Analysis  
  - Big O Notation and Time Complexity  
  - Sorting Algorithms (Bubble Sort, Merge Sort, Quick Sort)  
  - Greedy Algorithms  
- **Practice**: Analyze algorithm efficiency, implement sorting algorithms  
- **Applications**: Computer programming, optimization  

---

### 5. Combinatorics (Weeks 9-10)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Permutations and Combinations  
  - Binomial Theorem  
  - Pigeonhole Principle  
  - Counting Techniques (Inclusion-Exclusion Principle)  
- **Practice**: Solve combinatorial problems  
- **Applications**: Probability theory, cryptography  

---

### 6. Graph Theory (Weeks 11-12)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Graphs, Vertices, and Edges  
  - Types of Graphs (Directed, Undirected, Weighted)  
  - Graph Traversal Algorithms (BFS, DFS)  
  - Connectivity and Components  
  - Eulerian and Hamiltonian Paths  
- **Practice**: Implement graph traversal algorithms  
- **Applications**: Network theory, social network analysis  

---

### 7. Trees (Weeks 13-14)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Properties of Trees  
  - Binary Trees and Tree Traversals  
  - Binary Search Trees  
  - AVL Trees and Balanced Trees  
  - Tree Applications (Huffman Coding, Expression Trees)  
- **Practice**: Implement tree data structures and algorithms  
- **Applications**: Search algorithms, compression algorithms  

---

### 8. Boolean Algebra (Weeks 15-16)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Boolean Variables and Operations  
  - Truth Tables and Logic Circuits  
  - Simplification Techniques (Karnaugh Maps)  
  - Boolean Functions  
- **Practice**: Design logic circuits and simplify Boolean expressions  
- **Applications**: Circuit design, computer architecture  

---

### 9. Mathematical Induction (Weeks 17-18)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Principle of Mathematical Induction  
  - Strong Induction  
  - Applications in Proving Algorithms and Theorems  
- **Practice**: Use induction to prove mathematical statements  
- **Applications**: Algorithm correctness, mathematical proofs  

---

### 10. Recurrence Relations (Weeks 19-20)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Solving Linear Recurrence Relations  
  - Homogeneous and Non-Homogeneous Recurrences  
  - Divide-and-Conquer Recurrences  
  - Generating Functions  
- **Practice**: Solve recurrence relations and apply to algorithms  
- **Applications**: Algorithm analysis, dynamic programming  

---

### 11. Finite State Machines (Weeks 21-22)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Definitions of Finite Automata  
  - Deterministic vs. Non-Deterministic Finite Automata  
  - Applications in Language Recognition  
  - Regular Expressions  
- **Practice**: Design and simulate finite state machines  
- **Applications**: Compiler design, language processing  

---

### 12. Computability and Automata (Weeks 23-24)  
- **Topics**:  
  - Turing Machines  
  - Decidability and Computability  
  - NP-Complete Problems  
  - Complexity Classes  
- **Practice**: Analyze computational problems and their complexity  
- **Applications**: Theoretical computer science, algorithm design  


### Additional Syllabus Topics

#### 1. Speaking Mathematically  
- **Topics**:  
  - Variables: Fundamental to mathematical discourse and problem-solving.  
  - The Language of Sets: Essential for understanding set theory, relations, and functions.  
  - The Language of Relations and Functions: A key building block for later topics in algorithms and database theory.  

---

#### 2. The Logic of Compound Statements  
- **Topics**:  
  - Logical Form and Logical Equivalence: Understand logical structures, tautologies, and contradictions, foundational in proofs and reasoning.  
  - Conditional Statements: Key for logical structures like if-then, contrapositive, and biconditionals.  
  - Valid and Invalid Arguments: Learn important inference rules like Modus Ponens, Modus Tollens, and fallacies.  
  - Applications in Digital Logic Circuits: Connect theoretical logic to real-world computing applications, like Boolean algebra.  

---

#### 3. The Logic of Quantified Statements  
- **Topics**:  
  - Predicates and Quantified Statements I: Understanding universal and existential quantifiers, essential for proofs and formal logic.  
  - Predicates and Quantified Statements II: Focus on negations and their implications for logical reasoning.  
  - Statements with Multiple Quantifiers: Key to understanding complex logical structures in algorithms.  
  - Arguments with Quantified Statements: Learn how to prove the validity of logical arguments in formal systems.  

---

#### 4. Elementary Number Theory and Methods of Proof  
- **Topics**:  
  - Direct Proof and Counterexample: Master these techniques for effectively proving mathematical statements.  
  - Divisibility: Core to number theory and algorithm analysis.  
  - Indirect Argument: Contradiction and Contraposition: Learn indirect proof techniques for non-trivial statements.  
  - Applications in Algorithms: Connect proofs to algorithmic thinking, including the Euclidean algorithm and number theory.  

---

#### 5. Sequences, Mathematical Induction, and Recursion  
- **Topics**:  
  - Sequences: Critical for understanding recursive algorithms and combinatorics.  
  - Mathematical Induction I & II: Essential for proving properties of integers and sequences, widely used in algorithm analysis.  
  - Strong Mathematical Induction: Prove more complex mathematical properties.  
  - Applications: Correctness of Algorithms: Learn how to prove that algorithms function as expected.  

---

#### 6. Set Theory  
- **Topics**:  
  - Set Theory: Definitions and the Element Method of Proof: Establish a solid foundation in set theory, vital for understanding functions, relations, and databases.  
  - Properties of Sets: Essential for manipulating and proving properties about sets.  
  - Boolean Algebras: Learn Boolean operations, key to working with logic circuits and computer science.  

---

#### 7. Functions  
- **Topics**:  
  - Functions Defined on General Sets: Understand function properties, critical for programming and mathematical modeling.  
  - One-to-One and Onto, Inverse Functions: Fundamental to understanding injective, surjective, and bijective mappings.  
  - Cardinality with Applications to Computability: Connect set cardinality to computability, critical for understanding algorithmic complexity.  

---

#### 8. Relations  
- **Topics**:  
  - Relations on Sets: Fundamental for database theory and algorithm design.  
  - Reflexivity, Symmetry, and Transitivity: Learn these properties, important for equivalence relations and computer science applications.  
  - Modular Arithmetic with Applications to Cryptography: Connect number theory to cryptography.  
  - Partial Order Relations: Important for understanding ordered sets, relevant to scheduling, optimization, and databases.  

---

#### 9. Counting and Probability  
- **Topics**:  
  - Permutations and Combinations: Learn combinatorics for counting problems and algorithm complexity.  
  - The Pigeonhole Principle: A powerful tool in combinatorics, used in proofs and algorithm design.  
  - Probability Axioms and Expected Value: Learn probability theory basics, crucial for understanding algorithmic randomness and expected outcomes.  

---

#### 10. Graphs and Trees  
- **Topics**:  
  - Graphs: Definitions and Basic Properties: Learn graph theory terminology and concepts, essential for understanding network structures.  
  - Euler Circuits, Hamiltonian Circuits: Important for optimization and pathfinding algorithms.  
  - Trees: Key for hierarchical structures and efficient searching algorithms like binary search trees.  
  - Spanning Trees and Shortest Paths: Learn algorithms for minimum spanning trees and shortest paths, critical for network design and optimization.  

---

#### 11. Analysis of Algorithm Efficiency  
- **Topics**:  
  - O-, Ω-, and Θ-Notations: Master asymptotic notation for algorithm efficiency analysis.  
  - Applications: Analysis of Algorithm Efficiency I & II: Analyze and optimize algorithms such as search and sort.
